## 1. Central concept

> **Most people experience what software does. Perez is fascinated by how it works.**

The museum is a **courtyard building whose surfaces open.**
- Everything first presents a calm, finished surface: stone, bronze, glass, light.
- At chosen moments a surface **parts along a seam** and shows the working mechanism underneath. Each mechanism is drawn from the real architecture of Perez's projects.
- That one gesture, **Look Beneath the Surface**, is repeated at every scale:
  - the wordmark;
  - the central sculpture;
  - each flagship installation;
  - the floor of the Court itself, which opens onto the Laboratory below.

**Proposed narrative line** (a working line for the founder to approve or rewrite, not a quotation):

> *Curiosity made me explore. Ambition made me build.*

It maps onto the building:
- **Curiosity is downward.** The Foundations and the Laboratory sit beneath the surface: looking under things, taking them apart.
- **Ambition is upward.** The great stair rises past the Unfinished Wing to the Open Door on the roof, a horizon at dawn.

**How financial independence is expressed.** Through **authorship and self-reliance**, never money imagery:
- No coins, charts or gold.
- The building is self-built: its stair is still being extended.
- The final room is a door Perez made and holds open for others.

**Tone rules**
- Confident, quiet and precise.
- No melodrama, no "journey" clichés, no invented hardships.
- Personal content appears only where the founder supplies it.

## 3. Architectural identity: tropical brutalism with surreal moments

The building reads as a **plausible monument**: board-formed warm concrete, basalt, bronze. It is roughly in the lineage of post-independence Ghanaian modernism. **Surreal elements are rationed**, and each one means something:

| Impossible element | Where | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| The **Stratum** sculpture floats, its layers hovering apart | Centre of the Court | The core metaphor: a solid that can be opened |
| **Thresholds** whose far side cannot physically fit behind the wall | Every wing entrance | Lets each wing be its own world, and creates clean loading boundaries |
| A **cut ring in the Court floor** showing a whole room below | Around the sculpture's base | Literally "beneath the surface" |
| A **stair that keeps being built** past the roof | Great stair → Unfinished Wing | Ambition and unfinished work |
| A **free-standing door** on the roof plane, open to a horizon | The Open Door | Opportunity without walls |
| A **city inside a glass case** with its own sun | Accra We Dey | A world within a world |

**Seven architectural rules**
1. **One module.**
   - 1.2 m base module.
   - 3.6 m structural bay.
   - 0.4 m screen block.
   - All dimensions derive from these numbers, so procedural assembly still reads as authored.
2. **Mass first, then the cut.** Rooms are carved from heavy volumes. Openings are deep, so walls read 1.2–6 m thick.
3. **Controlled asymmetry.** Every room has one dominant axis and one deliberate break, such as an off-axis stair, a cantilever or a skewed beam.
4. **Light is the wayfinding.** Warm seam lines in floors and walls lead to wings. The oculus is always the orientation anchor.
5. **Rationed surrealism.** At most one impossible gesture per room.
6. **Honest structure.** Bronze armatures and scaffolds are detailed as if they could be built: bolts, plates, bearings.
7. **Quiet surfaces, loud mechanisms.** Architecture stays restrained. Only the machines carry detail and motion.

## 4. Ghanaian influences: references, origin and use

Every reference below is used for **architectural or material intelligence**, never as applied decoration.
- No kente patterns, Adinkra symbols or national colours are copied.
- [F] Perez should review this list. He may want to add, remove or reframe references from his own experience, and his judgement outranks this research.
- Items marked *[verify]* should be confirmed against primary sources before they appear in public copy.

| # | Reference | What it is | How the museum uses it | What we will not do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Asante Traditional Buildings** (northeast of Kumasi; UNESCO World Heritage List, 1980) | Compounds of rectangular rooms arranged around an open courtyard, built in timber, earth and thatch. Wall bases carry bas-relief | **The plan.** The museum is a courtyard: four Invention wings face a shared Court, like rooms around a compound courtyard. The courtyard is where life happens and every room is reached from it | Copy the bas-relief motifs, which carry specific meanings |
| 2 | **Ghanaian tropical modernism** (late 1950s–1960s). E.g. Independence Arch and Black Star Square, Accra (1961); KNUST campus buildings such as Unity Hall by John Owusu-Addo *[verify date]*; Fry & Drew's climate-led work and their book *Tropical Architecture in the Humid Zone* (1956). Surveyed in the V&A exhibition *Tropical Modernism: Architecture and Independence* (2024) | Monumental concrete, brise-soleil, deep overhangs, perforated screen walls, cross-ventilation | **The architectural language.** Board-formed concrete, deep reveals, a perforated screen wall that filters light into the Court, cantilevered platforms | Present the museum as a replica of any building |
| 3 | **Perforated screen blocks** (decorative concrete ventilation blocks, common across mid-century West African buildings) | Repeated cast blocks that shade and ventilate | **An original screen block** (§6) built as an instanced field. It gives patterned light and shadow at very low geometric cost | Reproduce a specific manufacturer's or building's block |
| 4 | **Narrow-strip weaving** (Asante and Ewe kente are woven in narrow strips, then sewn edge to edge into a cloth) | A *construction method*: many identical-width strips, each patterned independently, assembled into a whole | **A structural principle, not a pattern.** The Stratum sculpture is built from equal strata. The Court floor is laid in long bands. The UI uses a 12-band grid. "A body of work assembled strip by strip" | Use kente colours or named patterns, or call anything "kente-inspired" in public copy |
| 5 | **Earthen construction of the Upper East** (e.g. compounds around Sirigu; Kassena-Nankana and Frafra traditions) *[verify specifics]* | Hand-built earth walls, enclosed compounds, low thresholds, burnished plaster | **The Foundations** (Origin). Burnished earth-plaster walls and narrow, low passages that open into taller bays | Copy the wall paintings, which belong to their makers |
| 6 | **Larabanga Mosque** (Sudano-Sahelian earthen mosque, Savannah Region) | Timber members project permanently from the walls and serve as scaffolding for regular re-plastering | **The Unfinished Wing.** Permanent, beautifully detailed timber scaffolding: a building designed to be maintained and extended forever, never "finished" | Reproduce the mosque's form or any religious architecture |
| 7 | **Akan brass goldweights** (*abrammuo*), lost-wax cast | Small cast-brass weights used to measure gold dust in trade | **Material.** Cast, patinated bronze for machines and plaques. Small-scale "cast object" craft for Origin artefacts | Assign proverb meanings to our objects, or depict goldweights |
| 8 | **Odum timber** (Ghanaian name for *Milicia excelsa*, known internationally as iroko) *[verify]* | A durable West African hardwood | **Scaffold timber** in the Unfinished Wing and stair handrails | — |
| 9 | **Laterite** (iron-rich red earth common across Ghana) | Used in blocks, roads and plaster | **Warm aggregate** in the concrete and terrazzo, so "warm concrete" has a regional reason | Paint surfaces red |
| 10 | **Oware** (a mancala board game) | Already implemented in Perez's Accra We Dey game [V] | Used **only in the Accra We Dey exhibit**, because it is genuinely part of that project | Use it as general museum décor |

**Why this works.** The references shape the *plan* (courtyard), *language* (tropical modernism), *construction logic* (strip assembly), *materials* (laterite, bronze, odum) and *story* (permanent scaffolding).
- A Ghanaian visitor should recognise the intelligence behind them.
- A recruiter anywhere should simply experience a beautiful, coherent building.

## 6. Original pattern: the Seam Screen

[R] An original perforated screen block, so we never copy a protected or culturally specific design.

- **Block:** 400 × 400 × 120 mm cast concrete.
- **Aperture:** an off-centre L-shaped opening, with one leg wider than the other.
- **Field rule:** each block is placed with one of 4 rotations, chosen by a seeded rule (a Truchet-style tiling). The L openings join into long, meandering light paths across the wall: "seams" of light.
- **Signature use:** in the Court, the screen wall facing the dawn sky throws moving seam-shaped shadows across the floor.
- **Technical:**
  - one instanced mesh (about 60 tris);
  - its shadow pattern is baked into the floor lightmap;
  - on high tier only, a projected light cookie animates it.
- **Origin:** the pattern comes only from the museum's own seam idea and the screen-block typology (reference 3). It encodes no symbolic meaning, and we will not claim it does.

## 7. Design principles

1. **Understanding over spectacle.** Every motion must reveal something true about Perez or his work.
2. **Two registers, always.** Every reveal label has a *Plain* sentence for recruiters and a *Technical* line for engineers, switched by one global toggle.
3. **Truth is part of the design.** Status, role and team credit are designed elements (plaques), not footnotes.
4. **Never trap the visitor.** Contact and Quick View are one tap from anywhere. No forced intro. Every tour can be exited.
5. **Mobile is the original, desktop the enlargement.** Compositions are authored for portrait first.
6. **One museum.** Shared module, kit, materials and seam gesture across every room.
7. **Restraint in colour.** The architecture stays neutral and warm. Saturated colour appears only inside product screens and the Accra vitrine.
8. **Cultural depth without decoration.** Influences live in plan, structure and material.
9. **Physically plausible, selectively impossible.**
10. **Built to be maintained.** Every room can be regenerated from scripts. Content comes from data, not hard-coded scenes.
